Real options and valuation of flexibility

Energy assets are built in stages, run when they pay, mothballed when they don't, and expanded, switched or abandoned as prices move. Static NPV sees none of that. This course values flexibility. It covers why irreversibility and the ability to wait make investment an option, how to value options on project value with lattices, when to invest, enter, exit or mothball, and how to estimate the volatility that drives it all. It draws on Dixit and Pindyck for the theory, Copeland and Antikarov for practitioner method, Finnerty for a staged energy project, and Hull for the risk-neutral framework.

By the end you will be able to

  • Show why NPV and decision trees misprice flexibility, and value it by replication.
  • Value abandonment, expansion, contraction, deferral and staged options on a lattice.
  • Compute investment triggers and explain what moves them.
  • Compute entry and exit thresholds, explain hysteresis and value mothballing.
  • Estimate project volatility by simulation and structure staged-development cases.

How this course draws on the books

  • Theory of investment under uncertainty: Dixit & Pindyck ch.1–2, 5, 7. Dixit & Pindyck
  • Practitioner method and volatility: Copeland & Antikarov ch.4–6, 8–9. Copeland & Antikarov
  • Staged energy project: Finnerty ch.12. Finnerty
  • Risk-neutral valuation of real assets: Hull ch.36. Hull

A note on dates

Dixit and Pindyck (1994) and Copeland and Antikarov (2001) remain the standard references, and Hull (2021) and Finnerty (2013) are recent. Their examples predate the energy transition. Today carbon, policy and technology uncertainty, and expiring subsidies, often dominate the option values of energy projects. The Dated boxes flag these.

More depth in this course

Going-deeper sections cover the full cost of investing, payouts and early exercise, deriving β₁, why the hysteresis band is wide, and why Samuelson's theorem licenses a single tree. Tools include a wait-or-invest explorer, a multi-option lattice, an investment-trigger chart, an entry–exit band, and a consolidated-volatility simulator.

Modules

  1. NPV, decision trees and real options ≈ 55 min ·

    Copeland's four-step process, the marketed asset disclaimer, consolidated volatility by Monte Carlo, separated uncertainties and staged-development cases.
