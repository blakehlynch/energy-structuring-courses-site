Investment timing

Secondary≈ 55 min8 quiz questions4 flashcards

Learning objectives

  • Derive and apply the investment trigger V* = β/(β − 1)·I.
  • Explain value matching and smooth pasting.
  • Show how volatility, payout yield and rates move the trigger.
  • Explain why the NPV rule can be very wrong for irreversible projects.

Prerequisites

  • Modules 1–2; Course 6 Module 3 (GBM, Itô).

The basic model

Dixit and Pindyck's core model, due to McDonald and Siegel, asks when to pay a sunk cost \(I\) for a project whose value \(V\) follows geometric Brownian motion. The project's expected return is \(\mu\) and its payout (shortfall) rate is \(\delta\), so value grows at \(\alpha = \mu - \delta\). Dixit & Pindyck ch.5 §1 The investment opportunity is a perpetual American call on \(V\). Its value \(F(V)\) satisfies a differential equation in the waiting region, plus three boundary conditions: worthless at \(V=0\), equal to \(V^* - I\) at the trigger (value matching), and tangent to the payoff there (smooth pasting, \(F'(V^*)=1\)). Dixit & Pindyck ch.5 §2 The solution is

\[ F(V) = A V^{\beta_1}\ \ (V < V^*),\qquad V^* = \frac{\beta_1}{\beta_1 - 1}\, I \] \[ \beta_1 = \tfrac12 - \frac{r-\delta}{\sigma^2} + \sqrt{\left(\frac{r-\delta}{\sigma^2} - \tfrac12\right)^2 + \frac{2r}{\sigma^2}} \;>\; 1 \]

Because \(\beta_1 > 1\), the trigger always exceeds \(I\). Uncertainty and irreversibility drive a wedge between the NPV rule (invest when \(V > I\)) and the optimal rule. For realistic parameters the trigger is two or three times the investment cost, so the NPV rule is often very wrong rather than slightly wrong. Dixit & Pindyck ch.5 §1–2 The same answer comes from dynamic programming (using a subjective discount rate) and from contingent-claims analysis (when the risk is spanned by traded assets). Dixit & Pindyck ch.5 §2–3

Worked example 1 · the investment triggerChecked by code

A project costs $100. Its value is $100 today, with r = 5%, δ = 4% and σ = 25%.

β₁ = 1.6498 → multiple β₁/(β₁ − 1) = 2.539 → trigger V* = $253.89 option value today F(100) = $33.08, versus NPV = $0 trigger at σ = 15%: $185.88; at σ = 40%: $393.21

A break-even project is worth $33 as an option, and it should not be built until its value is about 2.5 times its cost. Doubling uncertainty from 15% to 40% more than doubles the required margin above cost. Dixit & Pindyck ch.5 §4

Investment trigger

Option value of a project against its NPV

I = $100. The option curve touches the NPV line tangentially at the trigger (smooth pasting). Below the trigger, waiting is worth more than investing.

What moves the trigger

  • Volatility up: β₁ falls, the trigger rises and the option is worth more. More uncertainty means more reason to wait. Dixit & Pindyck ch.5 §4
  • Payout rate δ up: waiting costs more forgone cash flow, so the trigger falls and investment comes sooner. As δ → 0 the option is never exercised. Dixit & Pindyck ch.5 §4
  • Interest rate r up (δ fixed): the trigger rises, so investment is deferred. The direction matches orthodox theory, but the channel is different: a higher rate makes holding the option cheaper relative to the deferred cost. Dixit and Pindyck argue that uncertainty often matters more than rates for investment timing, which is why they question interest-rate cuts as a stimulus. Dixit & Pindyck Preface; ch.5 §4
Worked example 2 · in the money but not yetChecked by code

Same parameters, but the project is now worth $150.

NPV = $50.00; option value F(150) = $64.59 → keep waiting

A 50% return over cost isn't enough when the value can still move a lot. The option to wait is worth more than the immediate gain. Dixit & Pindyck ch.5 §2

δ = 5% (r = 5%): trigger $216.26; δ = 8%: trigger $163.29

A project whose cash flows start large and early (high δ) is costly to delay. Hold the same project and the optimal policy changes, though its NPV hasn't. Dixit & Pindyck ch.5 §4 Synthesis

Using the rule in practice

The perpetual model is an idealisation. Real opportunities expire (licences, permits), competitors can pre-empt, and project value is rarely pure GBM. Commodity prices mean-revert (Course 6), and Dixit and Pindyck treat mean-reverting and jump processes as extensions. Dixit & Pindyck ch.5 §5 For finite windows, use the lattice from Module 2. The message carries over: hurdle rates or profitability indices above NPV = 0 are a rough way to price the option to wait, and firms that use high hurdles may be pricing irreversibility correctly by rule of thumb. Dixit & Pindyck ch.5 §4 Synthesis

ALM analogy

The trigger is the free boundary of a perpetual American call, the same mathematics as optimal exercise of a perpetual callable or the optimal lapse boundary in a behavioural model. Value matching and smooth pasting are the conditions you would impose on any optimal-exercise policy. The practical lesson is the same: waiting has value, and a payoff that is merely positive is not enough to exercise. Synthesis

Dated: parameters and rates

Dixit and Pindyck's numerical illustrations use early-1990s rates and costs. Today's higher rate environment after 2022, the volatility of power and carbon prices, and subsidy regimes (US tax credits, EU contracts for difference) all change the inputs, and subsidies that expire act as deadlines that cut the value of waiting. Outside the texts Rebuild β₁ from current curves and volatilities.

Going deeper: deriving β₁

In the waiting region the option earns only capital gains. Equating its expected return to the required return gives \(\tfrac12\sigma^2V^2F'' + (r-\delta)VF' - rF = 0\). Trying \(F = AV^{\beta}\) turns this into the quadratic \(\tfrac12\sigma^2\beta(\beta-1) + (r-\delta)\beta - r = 0\). Its positive root is β₁ > 1, and the condition F(0) = 0 rules out the negative root. Value matching and smooth pasting then fix A and V* together. Every model in Modules 3–4 has this structure: a homogeneous ODE, power solutions, and free boundaries set by matching and pasting. Dixit & Pindyck ch.5 §2

Key takeaways

  • Invest when value reaches V* = β₁/(β₁ − 1)·I, often two or three times the cost.
  • Value matching and smooth pasting define the optimal trigger.
  • Volatility raises the trigger, payouts lower it, and higher rates (δ fixed) raise it too.
  • For finite windows and real processes, use lattices. The perpetual rule gives the intuition.
