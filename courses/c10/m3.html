Storage lease

Capstone≈ 70 min8 quiz questions2 flashcards

Learning objectives

  • Compute intrinsic value from the forward curve and constraints.
  • Decide how much modelled extrinsic value to bid away.
  • Lock intrinsic and plan rolling-intrinsic operation.
  • Identify contract, operational and correlation risks.

Prerequisites

  • Courses 6 and 7.
The brief

A gas utility auctions a one-year lease (April to March) of 2 Bcf (2,000,000 MMBtu) of reservoir storage. It injects up to 20,000 MMBtu a day and withdraws up to 40,000, with $0.02 variable cost each way, and runs empty to empty. Bidders quote a fixed fee. The previous lessee paid $2.3m. How much should you bid, and how will you run the lease? Synthesis

1. Intrinsic value: what you can lock today

Intrinsic value is the best schedule against today's curve, found by optimisation and locked with forward trades. It doesn't depend on any model. Swindle ch.13 (p.309) Eydeland and Wolyniec's forward optimisation does exactly this. Once the hedges are on, the cash flow is fixed whatever prices do, though physical decisions still follow the spreads at the time. Eydeland & Wolyniec ch.8 (pp.355–359)

Worked example 1 · the bidChecked by code

The forward curve runs from $3.20 (April) to $4.60 (January). Your model puts extrinsic value at 15% of intrinsic. You will bid half of the extrinsic and keep a $0.15m margin for operating and model risk.

intrinsic $2.436m; modelled extrinsic $0.365m bid = 2.436 + 0.5 × 0.365 − 0.15 = $2.469m = $1.234/MMBtu of capacity paying last year's $2.3m would leave an expected $0.319m before your $0.15m margin

Bid off intrinsic plus a fraction of extrinsic, never the full model value. Swindle stresses that extrinsic value rests on correlation inputs the market barely reveals. Rolling intrinsic is only a lower-bound strategy, and its gains are offset by transaction costs. Swindle ch.13 (pp.331–333) Mahoney §3.1.4 (pp.70–71) Synthesis

Storage lease bid

Intrinsic value plus the share of extrinsic you are willing to pay for

2 Bcf, 20,000/day injection, 40,000/day withdrawal, $0.02 each way, empty to empty. The spread scale stretches the base curve around April's $3.20.

Worked example 2 · a narrower seasonal spreadChecked by code

If the winter–summer spread halves, intrinsic value falls to $1.178m and the bid to $1.116m. Last year's $2.3m fee would now lose money.

Lease values track the curve's seasonal spread. When markets are oversupplied with storage, lessors must accept lower fees, and Swindle notes that storage owners charge what the spread will bear. Swindle ch.2 (p.28) Synthesis

2. Read the contract like a structurer

Leases differ in what the lessor keeps. Eydeland and Wolyniec's example lease requires the lessee to be 75% full by end-October, keeps a 30% winter minimum, and lets the lessor withdraw up to 75% at its discretion. Each constraint cuts value, and lessor withdrawals driven by its own customer demand turn part of the deal into a load obligation. Eydeland & Wolyniec ch.8 (p.354) Physical facts matter too: ratchets (rates that fall as inventory nears full or empty), maintenance windows and fuel retention all reduce deliverability. Swindle notes that the geology itself is only partly known. Swindle ch.13 (pp.298–299)

3. Run the lease

  • Lock intrinsic on award: buy the injection months and sell the withdrawal months on the curve. Eydeland & Wolyniec ch.8 (p.359)
  • Re-optimise: roll the hedge when the curve offers a better schedule (rolling intrinsic). Track the gains against the extrinsic you paid for. Swindle ch.13 (pp.323–324)
  • Use the daily market: intra-month cash spreads and weather-driven spikes add value that monthly models miss, and cold weather in consuming regions drives the biggest daily moves. Swindle ch.13 (p.331)
  • Watch the greeks of any CSO overlay: LP re-optimisation makes them jump. Swindle ch.13 (p.326)

4. Term sheet sketch

  • Capacity, rates and ratchets, inventory start and end conditions, and any intermediate minimums or maximums. Swindle ch.13 (p.299)
  • Fee: fixed, paid monthly. Consider a lower fixed fee plus a share of realised spread above a hurdle if the lessor will share upside. Synthesis
  • Fuel retention and variable charges, maintenance windows, force majeure and curtailment priority against other users. Outside the texts
  • Lessor recall or peaking rights explicitly priced, or removed. Eydeland & Wolyniec ch.8 (p.354)
ALM analogy

Bidding for storage is like bidding for an asset portfolio with an embedded rebalancing option. The intrinsic part is a locked spread, like a matched book. The extrinsic part is the value of dynamic rebalancing, which depends on correlation assumptions as a dynamic hedging strategy's value depends on the covariance matrix. Pay for the locked spread and only a cautious share of the model value. Synthesis

Dated: storage markets today

European storage now operates under minimum fill obligations introduced in 2022, which change both value and flexibility. US seasonal spreads have narrowed in some years with ample production and more salt-cavern capacity, while weather extremes still create large daily spikes. Outside the texts Check AGSI+ (GIE), EIA weekly storage and current tariff filings.

Going deeper: who should hold storage?

The utility leases its storage because it values certainty of a fee above the volatile trading value of the facility. You lease it because you can monetise spreads across a portfolio, hedge efficiently and use daily optionality. Eydeland and Wolyniec's principle that liquid markets make values additive cuts both ways. If the utility can buy gas at liquid hubs in winter, it doesn't need storage for supply security and should lease it out. If the location is illiquid, joint use with the utility's supply needs is worth up to the bid-ask spread, and the lease should share that value. Eydeland & Wolyniec ch.8 (pp.381–383) Synthesis

Key takeaways

  • Compute intrinsic value and lock it. Bid only a fraction of modelled extrinsic value.
  • Lease values follow the seasonal spread, so a fee that worked last year may not this year.
  • Constraints, ratchets and lessor rights all cut value. Price them or remove them.
  • Run the lease with rolling intrinsic and daily optimisation, and watch transaction costs.
