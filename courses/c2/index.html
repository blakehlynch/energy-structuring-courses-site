Course 2 · Foundation

Refining economics, crack spreads and crude differentials

Why does one crude sell for $9 a barrel less than another loaded the same day? Why does a refinery keep running when its accounts show a loss? What exactly does a refiner sell when it signs a tolling agreement? This course answers those questions by following the barrel through the refinery and back out into prices. You'll finish able to value a crude grade at a specific plant and reason about the margin and differential risks that physical originators take on.

By the end you will be able to

  • Read a crude assay and blend crudes correctly.
  • Convert product prices across units and reason about blending to spec.
  • Classify a refinery, compute its Nelson index and value its incremental barrel.
  • Compute margins and cracks, design a crack hedge, and value operating flexibility.
  • Compute value-in-use and breakeven differentials, and explain what moves market differentials.

How this course draws on the books

  • Backbone: Kaiser et al. (refining economics, complexity, prices) and Long (the trader's view of products, refining and margins). Kaiser Long
  • Orientation: Downey's assay and refining chapters. Downey
  • Markets and hedging: Edwards and Schofield. Edwards Schofield
  • Structuring and finance link: Clews on refinery risks and tolling; Mahoney and Swindle as previews of tolling valuation. Clews Mahoney Swindle
  • Commercial practice: Trafigura's guide on blending and super-refineries. Trafigura CD

Where this course sits

This is a Foundation course in your reading list's sequence, following Oil 101 and the Oil Trading Manual and built mainly on Kaiser et al. Course 1 (petroleum fundamentals) gives the macro context. Course 3 (physical oil trading) builds on Module 5's marker-plus-differential pricing. Course 7 turns Module 4's spread-option view of a refinery into full tolling valuation, and Course 9 finances tolled assets.

Modules

  1. Crude quality and the assay ≈ 50 min ·
    API gravity and sulfur, reading an assay, what blends linearly, and why the assay is an estimate. Interactive crude blending lab.
  2. Refined products and specifications ≈ 55 min ·
    The product barrel, key specs per product, unit conversions, and blending to spec. Interactive gasoline blending optimiser.
  3. Refinery configuration and complexity ≈ 55 min ·
    Separation, conversion and finishing; refinery types; the Nelson index; the incremental barrel. Interactive refinery builder.
  4. Refining margins and crack spreads ≈ 70 min ·
    GPW, margins and netbacks; crack spreads and their drivers; hedging; the refinery as a spread option; tolling. Crack calculator and hedge simulator.
  5. Crude differentials and value-in-use ≈ 60 min ·
    Marker plus differential, value-in-use at a refinery, differential sensitivities, and what sets market differentials. Value-in-use calculator.
