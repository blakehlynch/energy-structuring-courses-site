Course 5 · Foundation

Electricity market design and price formation

Power prices are formed very differently from oil prices. A non-storable product must balance every second over a shared network, demand hardly responds to price, and a single operator runs the real-time market. This course explains how wholesale electricity markets are built: the merit order and the technology mix, day-ahead and real-time markets, nodal prices and transmission rights, scarcity pricing and capacity markets, ancillary services, renewables and storage, and market power. It draws mainly on Kirschen and Strbac and on Stoft, with Murray and Edwards for practice.

By the end you will be able to

  • Read a load-duration curve and compute capacity factors, screening curves and a least-cost mix.
  • Clear a merit order, settle a two-settlement position and value a CfD.
  • Compute locational prices, congestion rent and FTR payoffs.
  • Explain scarcity pricing, missing money and capacity mechanisms, and value storage arbitrage.
  • Assess market power with Lerner, HHI and pivotal-supplier tests.

How this course draws on the books

  • Market mechanics and networks: Kirschen & Strbac ch.1–8. Kirschen & Strbac
  • Economics, reliability and market power: Stoft Parts 1–5. Stoft
  • Structures and UK practice: Murray. Murray
  • Trading view of the stack: Edwards ch.4.2. Edwards

A note on dates

Kirschen and Strbac (2019) are current on concepts. Stoft (2002) and Murray (2009) describe markets that have since been reformed many times. Treat their rules as illustrations and check the current manuals of the market you work in. Each module's Dated box lists what to verify.

More depth in this course

Going-deeper sections cover demand inelasticity as the root of design choices, screening curves for net load, the covariance cost of intermittent sellers, FTRs as options on scarcity, risk and capacity mechanisms, revenue stacking for storage, and forward contracts as competition policy. Tools include a load-duration builder, screening curves, a two-settlement calculator, a two-zone LMP model, a missing-money calculator, a battery arbitrage model and a withholding simulator.

Modules

  1. Physics and industry structure ≈ 50 min ·
    Why electricity differs from other commodities, power versus energy, load-duration curves and the main market structures.
  2. Cost curves, screening curves and merit order ≈ 60 min ·
    Fixed and variable costs, screening curves, least-cost mix, merit order and uniform pricing.
  3. Day-ahead, real-time and two-settlement ≈ 60 min ·
    Market sequence, unit commitment and uplift, the managed spot market, two-settlement, virtual bids and CfDs.
  4. Congestion, nodal pricing and FTRs ≈ 65 min ·
    Congestion and locational prices, nodal prices in meshed networks, congestion surplus, FTRs and flowgate rights.
  5. Scarcity, VOLL and capacity markets ≈ 60 min ·
    Price spikes and fixed-cost recovery, VOLL pricing, caps and missing money, capacity payments, markets and reliability options.
  6. Ancillary services, renewables and storage ≈ 60 min ·
    Reliability resources, opportunity-cost pricing of reserves, renewables' market effects and capture rates, storage arbitrage.
  7. Market power ≈ 55 min ·
    Definition, Lerner and Cournot models, HHI, withholding, forward contracts, monitoring and mitigation.
