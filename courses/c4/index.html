Course 4 · Core

Trade and structured commodity finance

Physical trading runs on borrowed money. This course explains how trading houses and producers are financed against the commodities they own, produce or sell: letters of credit and guarantees, inventory and borrowing-base lines, pre-export finance and trader-originated prepayments, insurance, export credit agency cover, receivables sales and securitisation. It ends with the regulatory and fraud risks that decide whether a deal can be booked at all. For an ALM actuary much of the toolkit will feel familiar: haircuts, cover ratios, subordination, capital charges and liquidity stress, applied to cargoes instead of bonds.

By the end you will be able to

  • Map a trading house's funding stack and stress its liquidity.
  • Choose payment and LC structures and cost them.
  • Structure inventory, borrowing-base and reserve-based facilities.
  • Size and stress a prepayment and assign its risks.
  • Pick credit enhancements, receivables solutions and capital-efficient structures, and pass compliance gates.

How this course draws on the books

  • Structured commodity finance: Moors & Rutten (techniques, cases, insurance, ECAs). Moors & Rutten
  • Legal and structuring practice: Wynne (structures, warehouses, security, regulation, workouts). Wynne
  • The trader's view: Trafigura's guides on funding and prepayments. Trafigura CD Trafigura PD
  • Oil-trading credit and RBL: Long ch.13, Clews ch.14–17. Long Clews

A note on dates

Wynne reflects English law and regulation as of 2012, Rutten the market of 2013–14, and the Trafigura guides 2018–20. Sanctions (above all on Russia since 2022), Basel III implementation, the 2020 trader frauds and the move to electronic documents all postdate the texts. Each module flags what to verify.

More depth in this course

Going-deeper sections cover the economics of trader-originated credit, setting advance rates, risk allocation in prepayments, matching enhancements to risks, borrowing against versus selling receivables, and a compliance-first origination checklist. Tools include a funding-needs stress model, an LC discounting calculator, a borrowing-base calculator, a prepayment cash waterfall, a hedge overlay, a securitisation loss model and a capital-return calculator.

Modules

  1. How trading houses are funded ≈ 50 min ·
    Self-liquidating lending, the funding stack, working capital and margin liquidity, structured commodity finance versus project finance.
  2. Payment methods, LCs and guarantees ≈ 55 min ·
    Open account to advance payment, LC mechanics and costs, LC variants for traders, standbys, discounting deferred payments.
  3. Inventory, warehouse and borrowing-base finance ≈ 60 min ·
    Warehouse receipt finance, collateral management, fraud cases, ownership and repo structures, borrowing bases and reserve-based lending.
  4. Pre-export finance and prepayments ≈ 65 min ·
    PXF versus prepayment, limited recourse, trader-originated deals, cover and breakeven, top-ups, accounts, SPVs and the Cocobod and Sonangol histories.
  5. Security and credit enhancement ≈ 60 min ·
    Security packages, political and credit insurance, embedded price hedges (Ashanti, GNPC), ECAs and development banks.
  6. Receivables, forfaiting and securitisation ≈ 55 min ·
    Factoring, reverse factoring, forfaiting, trader and producer securitisations, true sale and loss layers.
  7. Regulation, sanctions, fraud and workouts ≈ 55 min ·
    Fraud patterns, bribery, sanctions and AML, bank capital for trade finance, waivers, workouts and enforcement.
