Benchmarks and price reporting

Core≈ 60 min9 quiz questions11 flashcards

Learning objectives

  • Explain why most oil is priced by formula against a few spot benchmarks.
  • Distinguish PRA assessments from futures settlements and read methodology risks.
  • Describe the Brent complex (forward, Dated, CFDs, futures) and WTI and Dubai roles.
  • Compute basket formula prices and quality escalators.
  • Decompose a grade's differential into quality and freight.

Prerequisites

  • Course 1 (curve, inventories) and Course 2 (value-in-use) recommended.

Why a few prices set the rest

Most physical oil moves under term contracts, but very little of it is priced at a fixed number agreed in advance. Instead, a cargo is priced by formula: an agreed benchmark, averaged over agreed days, plus or minus a negotiated differential. Downey ch.17 Long §3.1 Spot deals are a small share of volume, but they are the barrels sold to whoever bids highest for prompt delivery. Their prices therefore carry the latest news and become the reference that term barrels follow. Downey ch.17 Trafigura CD ch.10

Long traces the shift. Until the 1980s most international crude sold at fixed or official prices that changed rarely. When prices became volatile after 1979, whichever side was losing on a fixed price was tempted to walk away. Floating, benchmark-linked formulas removed that temptation, and paper markets (forwards, futures, swaps) then made the floating price hedgeable. Long §3.1, §3.2.2

ALM analogy

A formula-priced cargo is like a floating-rate loan: benchmark plus spread. The benchmark (Dated Brent, WTI, Dubai) plays the role of the reference rate. The differential is the negotiated spread for quality, location and timing, much as a loan spread prices credit and liquidity. Basis risk between benchmarks resembles the mismatch between two reference rates, and benchmark reform in oil (baskets widened, methodologies changed) echoes the IBOR transition. Synthesis

Where benchmark prices come from

Downey identifies two sources of the daily snapshot: futures exchanges, whose settlement prices come from trading near the close, and price reporting agencies (PRAs), chiefly Platts and Argus, which assess over-the-counter trade. Downey ch.17 Many PRA assessments are built from a short end-of-day pricing window in which reporters watch bids, offers and deals closely. Traders then use the mean of the published high and low. Downey ch.17

  • A PRA price is an assessment, not a trade. Long stresses that a published price need not mean that a given cargo could have traded there that day. By contrast, a futures "last traded" price is a real deal, but possibly for one 1,000 bbl lot. Long §3.5.1
  • Methodology matters. Which specification, location, cut-off time and roll date does the quote represent? Comparing Dubai and WTI at month-end can mix delivery months because the two roll on different dates. Long §3.5.2
  • Thinly traded grades are assessed as a differential to more liquid ones, so judgement and the risk of selective deal reporting creep in. Long §3.5.3

Trafigura calls the PRAs private companies performing a public role: the spot market is a small fraction of trade, yet it sets prices for much larger volumes. Trafigura CD ch.10

The Brent complex

The North Sea marker is a family of linked contracts rather than one price. Downey Appendix One Schofield ch.5

  • Forward ("cash") BFOE and Platts MOC partials. OTC contracts for 600,000 bbl cargoes loading in a future month. In the Platts Market-on-Close (MOC) assessment window, participants trade standardised 100,000 bbl "partials". When one counterparty accumulates six partials (6 × 100 kbbl = 600 kbbl) with the same seller for the same month, they converge into a full physical cargo that the seller must declare. Dubai and Oman work the same way with 25,000 bbl partials, 20 of which (500 kbbl) make a cargo. The seller then nominates a 3-day loading window with contract notice (traditionally month-ahead / 25-day notice, and 10 days to month-ahead for modern Dated Brent). Positions are passed along daisy chains, and traders who do not want physical crude can be "clocked" if they hold the contract past the notice deadline. Downey Appendix One Long §3.1 Trafigura CD ch.10 Synthesis
  • Dated Brent. Once a loading window is assigned, the contract is "dated": a physical cargo. Platts assesses Dated Brent on cargoes loading in a forward window (historically 10–21 days ahead, expanded in the modern market to a 10-day to month-ahead delivery window including CIF Rotterdam Midland deliveries). Dated is therefore really a short forward, not immediate delivery. Downey Appendix One Schofield ch.5
  • Grades and squeezes. As Brent Blend output fell, Forties and Oseberg (2002) and then Ekofisk were added so that no one could corner the deliverable supply. The seller delivers the cheapest grade, so quality escalators such as the Forties sulphur de-escalator keep it fair. Downey Appendix One Long §3.3.4
  • CFDs and DFLs. These are swaps on the gap between Dated and the forward or futures. They let a trader whose cargo will be priced in a particular week hedge that week's Dated exposure (Module 3). Schofield ch.5 Downey Appendix One
  • ICE Brent futures are cash-settled against an index of forward trades, with EFPs linking futures to physical. Schofield ch.5 Downey Appendix One
Worked example 0 · Platts MOC partials convergenceChecked by code

During the Platts MOC window for next-month Cash BFOE, Trader A lifts offers on several partials, seven of them from Trader B. In Dubai the same day, Trader A buys 28 partials from Trader C.

Brent: partials per cargo = 600 kbbl / 100 kbbl = 6 converged with Trader B = floor(7 / 6) = 1 cargo (600,000 bbl), 1 partial still open Dubai: partials per cargo = 500 kbbl / 25 kbbl = 20 converged with Trader C = floor(28 / 20) = 1 cargo, 8 partials still open

Trader B, as seller, must now declare the cargo: the loading terminal, laycan and deliverable grade (Brent, Forties, Oseberg, Ekofisk, Troll or WTI Midland). Partials that have not converged are not cash settled. They stay open as forward positions that can converge with later trades against the same counterparty or be closed out. Downey Appendix One Outside the texts Synthesis

WTI, Dubai and official selling prices

  • WTI (NYMEX) delivers ratably over a month at Cushing, Oklahoma, an inland hub. That is why traders compare coastal grades against Light Louisiana Sweet rather than WTI when checking transatlantic arbitrage. Downey Appendix One The Cushing constraint resurfaced in 2020 (Course 1, Module 3).
  • Dubai and Oman anchor Middle East sour crude sold to Asia. Gulf producers set official selling prices (OSPs) each month as differentials to these and other markers. The OSP varies by destination and is sometimes set retroactively. Long §3.2.2 Downey ch.17
  • Formula baskets mix several markers to dilute any single one's quirks or manipulation risk. Downey gives Mexican export formulas that weight US Gulf grades, fuel oil and Dated Brent. Downey ch.17 Long §3.3.4

Physical crude differs from the marker in three dimensions, and each has a price: quality (gravity, sulphur, metals, acidity: Course 2's value-in-use), location (freight to the refining centre) and timing (curve structure between pricing dates). Long §3.2.1 Trafigura CD ch.8

Worked example 1 · a basket formula priceChecked by code

A producer prices an export grade at 0.4 × WTS + 0.4 × LLS + 0.2 × Dated Brent − $6.35. These weights and constant are illustrative, in the style of the formulas Downey reports. On the pricing day: WTS $76.20, LLS $79.10, Dated $80.40.

0.4 × 76.20 + 0.4 × 79.10 + 0.2 × 80.40 − 6.35 = $71.85/bbl

The constant is the commercial lever. The producer revises it monthly to keep the grade competitive, much as an OSP differential is revised. Downey ch.17

Worked example 2 · a sulphur de-escalatorChecked by code

A forward contract delivers Forties at 0.82% sulphur. The de-escalator applies above 0.6% at an illustrative 30 cents per 0.1% (the actual rate is set by Platts after consultation; Downey describes the mechanism).

(0.82 − 0.60) / 0.1 × $0.30 = $0.66/bbl deducted → invoice at $80.40 − 0.66 = $79.74

Because the seller chooses which grade to deliver, the buyer is protected only to the extent that escalators price the quality gap correctly. Downey Appendix One

Worked example 3 · where a grade's differential comes fromChecked by code

A West African light sweet crude competes with the marker for the same European refiners. Refiners value its quality at $0.90/bbl more than the marker (from a value-in-use model, Course 2). Freight to Rotterdam is $1.10/bbl from the North Sea but $2.60 from West Africa. With the marker at $80.00 FOB:

grade FOB value = 80.00 + 0.90 + 1.10 − 2.60 = $79.40 → differential to marker = −0.60

A better quality can still trade at a discount if it sits further from the buyers. If European margins weaken or Asian ones strengthen, the grade's best home changes and its differential follows (Module 6). Synthesis Long §3.2.1 Downey ch.17

Dated: benchmark mechanics

The books describe benchmarks as of roughly 2008–2014. Since then several changes, all outside the texts, need checking against current exchange and PRA documentation: Troll and later WTI Midland were added to the Dated Brent basket (WTI Midland from 2023 deliveries); ICE Brent futures expiry rules and forward notice periods changed; the Dubai Mercantile Exchange was rebranded; Murban futures launched in Abu Dhabi (2021) and Shanghai crude futures (2018); and NYMEX floor trading ended. PRAs now operate under IOSCO's oil-PRA principles (2012). Outside the texts Check Platts and Argus methodology guides, ICE and CME contract specifications, and IOSCO's PRA reviews.

Going deeper: why the market trades differentials

Downey describes a family tree of spreads: crude markers linked to each other by arbitrage spreads, products linked to crude by cracks, and each grade linked to its marker by a differential. Traders rarely want flat-price exposure. Trafigura describes systematically hedging it, which leaves basis (the differential) as the risk the desk actually runs and earns from. Downey ch.18 Trafigura CD ch.10 That is why the commercial skill in physical trading lies in valuing differentials: knowing what a grade is worth relative to the marker in each possible destination, and when that will change. Synthesis

A second point concerns benchmark integrity. A thin benchmark is easy to squeeze: buy all deliverable cargoes and the price you are long rises. Long's remedies are baskets of grades, averaging over several days, and using several publications. Each widens the deliverable supply or the pricing sample, at the cost of more basis risk for users. Long §3.3.1, §3.3.4, §3.5 Downey Appendix One

Key takeaways

  • Most oil is priced by formula: benchmark average plus differential. The spot market is small but sets the reference.
  • Benchmarks come from futures settlements and PRA assessments. Know each methodology's specification, location, timing and roll.
  • Brent is a complex: forward BFOE, Dated (a short forward), CFDs, futures and EFPs. WTI is inland at Cushing, and Dubai and Oman anchor Asian sour crude through OSPs.
  • Differentials price quality, location and timing. The desk's real exposure and skill is in differentials, not flat price.
