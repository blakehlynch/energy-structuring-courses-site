Course 3 · Core

Physical oil trading: benchmarks, pricing, contracts, freight

This course follows a cargo from the pricing formula to the bill of lading. It asks how oil is priced, hedged, contracted, shipped, routed and paid for, and where the money is made or lost along the way. The backbone is Long's Oil Trading Manual, with Downey's market chapters, Schofield on the Brent complex, Stopford on shipping and Trafigura's description of how a modern trading house works.

By the end you will be able to

  • Read and draft a benchmark-linked pricing formula and spot the options inside it.
  • Hedge a physical deal with futures, swaps and CFDs, and isolate basis risk.
  • Allocate costs and risk under Incoterms and build a laytime and demurrage statement.
  • Price freight in Worldscale, compute TCE and judge an arbitrage on its full cost stack.
  • Choose credit security for a counterparty and understand the operations that protect the margin.

How this course draws on the books

  • Pricing and paper: Long ch.3, Downey ch.17–19 and Appendix One, Schofield ch.5. Long Downey Schofield
  • Contracts, operations and credit: Long ch.12, 13 and 16. Long
  • Freight: Stopford ch.5–6, Downey ch.11. Stopford
  • The trading house: Trafigura, Commodities Demystified, ch.4–10. Trafigura CD

A note on dates

Long's manual reflects 1996–2002 practice and Downey's market chapters about 2009. The mechanics (formulas, averaging, EFPs, laytime, Worldscale, LCs) are still how the business works. Contract specifications, benchmark baskets, regulation and trade flows have changed, and each module's amber Dated box lists what to verify against current exchange, PRA and ICC documentation.

More depth in this course

Each module ends with a Going deeper section: embedded options in pricing clauses, minimum-variance hedge ratios, demurrage chain mismatches, the economics of sailing speed, destination optionality, and expected credit loss against the cost of security. Interactive tools include a differential builder, a pricing-window simulator, a hedge P&L decomposer, an Incoterms map, a laytime calculator, a voyage and TCE model, a destination comparator and a credit exposure profile.

Modules

  1. Benchmarks and price reporting ≈ 60 min ·
    Formula pricing, PRAs and futures settlements, the Brent complex, WTI and Dubai, OSPs, and where differentials come from.
  2. Pricing formulas and windows ≈ 60 min ·
    Formula anatomy, mean of means, averaging windows, fixed versus back-to-back, pricing-period mismatch and its hedge, EFPs, embedded options.
  3. Forward, futures and paper markets ≈ 70 min ·
    Futures and margin, swaps and Asian settlement, hedging and basis, Brent CFD locks, EFP/EFS, bookouts, hedge ratios.
  4. Contracts, Incoterms, laytime and demurrage ≈ 60 min ·
    Contract formation and key terms, Incoterms and risk transfer, nominations, NOR, laytime and demurrage at two levels.
  5. Tanker freight and Worldscale ≈ 60 min ·
    Tanker classes, charter types and cost allocation, Worldscale, TCE, FFAs, vessel arbitrage, speed and the cube rule.
  6. Arbitrage and netbacks ≈ 55 min ·
    Arbitrage economics, netbacks, delivered cost stacks, breakeven freight, destination optionality.
  7. Operations, credit and risk control ≈ 60 min ·
    The operator's role, inspection, B/Ls and LOIs, credit assessment and security, exposure profiles, desk controls.
