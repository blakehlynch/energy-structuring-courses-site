Pricing formulas and windows

Prerequisites

  • Module 1.

Anatomy of a pricing formula

Long's chapter on pricing arrangements is the backbone of this module. Before any formula, a contract must pin down what is being priced: guaranteed quality and escalators, the delivery date range, location and Incoterm, currency and unit, duties and credit terms. A price is meaningless without them. Long §3.2.1 A floating formula then specifies:

  1. The reference. A PRA assessment (e.g. mean of the Platts high and low), a futures settlement, or a basket. For products, a common reference is the "mean of means" of two related quotes, such as cargoes CIF NWE and barges FOB ARA. Long §3.3.1, §3.4.1
  2. The pricing period. The B/L date only, B/L ±2 days (five quotes), five days after B/L, a month's average, or for crude often 11 quotes around the B/L date. Averaging reduces exposure to a one-day spike and to manipulation. Long §3.3.1
  3. The differential. Fixed when the deal is struck, or floating (for example, the PRA's own published differential for that grade on the pricing date). Long §3.3.1–3.3.3
  4. Adjustments: quality escalators and de-escalators, and sometimes date-based adjustments for late loading. Long §3.2.1

Variants include trigger pricing, where one side chooses when to fix against the futures screen. Its popularity waned because the trigger holder could influence the marker price just before pulling the trigger. Feedstocks are often priced as percentages of other quotes, for example vacuum gasoil as a share of gasoline plus a share of gasoil, because they are not quoted outright. Long §3.3.2, §3.3.4 Some producers offer buyers flexible pricing windows (before or after B/L, or a month's average) as a commercial incentive, so buyers can exploit contango or backwardation. Long §3.2.1

Worked example 1 · mean of means and a five-day averageChecked by code

A gasoil cargo is priced at the mean of means of two quotes, averaged over B/L ±2 days. On the B/L date the quotes are cargoes $712–716/t and barges $718–721/t. These numbers are illustrative; Long works the same arithmetic on a Russian gasoil deal.

mean of each quote: 714.00 and 719.50 → mean of means = $716.75/t five daily means of means: 716.75, 709.50, 712.25, 703.00, 706.50 → average = $709.60/t

Contracts should also say what happens if a publication day is missing, for example a holiday. Long flags this as a common gap. Long §3.4.3

Fixed or floating, and the trader's choice

Long's Russian gasoil example captures the core decision. A trader has bought a cargo at a formula (quote on B/L plus a premium). Before loading, they can sell it either at a fixed price, which takes a view that the market will fall before B/L, or back-to-back on the same formula plus a higher premium, which locks the margin whatever the market does. Long §3.4.1

Worked example 2 · fixed sale versus back-to-backChecked by code

Bought 30,000 t at the B/L-date mean of means + $2.25/t. Offers available before loading: a fixed $731/t, or back-to-back at the mean of means + $3.00. The trader sells fixed. On the B/L date the mean of means is $722.40. All numbers are illustrative.

cost = 722.40 + 2.25 = $724.65/t fixed sale margin = 731.00 − 724.65 = $6.35/t → $190,500 back-to-back margin = 3.00 − 2.25 = $0.75/t → $22,500, whatever the market did

The fixed sale paid off because the market fell. Had it risen by $10, the same decision would have lost money. The back-to-back margin is small but certain. A fixed sale is an outright price position that a disciplined desk would hedge with futures or swaps, or record as a deliberate view within limits (Module 7). Long §3.4.1 Trafigura CD ch.10

Fixed prices have not vanished. End users such as a heating oil distributor or an airline want budget certainty. Long notes that the swaps market grew to give them a fixed price while their physical supply stays floating with a familiar supplier. Long §3.1, §3.4.4

Pricing-period mismatch

A trader who buys on one pricing period and sells on another carries the benchmark's movement between the two periods, even though both legs are "floating". Trafigura's example has a purchase priced at the benchmark on loading and a sale priced weeks later on delivery. To neutralise this, the trader sells futures as the purchase prices and buys them back as the sale prices, leaving only basis risk. Trafigura CD ch.10 Refiners do the reverse: they price crude over the same period as product sales to lock a margin. Long §3.3

ALM analogy

This is a repricing gap. An asset that resets on one date funded by a liability that resets on another leaves rate exposure in between, even though both are floating. The fix is the same in both worlds: a strip of futures or forwards over the gap. Daily averaging windows are the oil equivalent of averaging resets. Synthesis

Worked example 3 · a falling market between two windowsChecked by code

A cargo is bought at the marker average over B/L ±2 days minus $0.50 and sold at the average over discharge ±2 days plus $0.70: $1.20 of differential margin on paper. The voyage takes about 25 trading days. In the simulated path that the tool below starts with, the marker drifts lower.

purchase price (avg over B/L window − 0.50) = $81.45 sale price (avg over discharge window + 0.70) = $77.64 unhedged margin = $−3.81/bbl futures hedge (sold through purchase window, bought back through sale window) = +$4.99 hedged margin = $1.18/bbl ≈ the $1.20 differential, less a little basis between futures and the physical marker

The hedge converts a flat-price gamble into the differential margin the trader actually negotiated. Trafigura CD ch.10 Synthesis

Pricing through futures: EFPs

An EFP prices a physical cargo at an agreed differential to futures by transferring futures positions between buyer and seller. The physical buyer hands the seller a long futures position at the agreed level. Each side has already built or will unwind its futures in its own time, so each effectively prices the cargo when it chooses, often in small lots that don't move the screen. Long §3.3.5 Downey Appendix One In Long's gasoil example, the buyer triggers an EFP at a level that leaves the seller long futures. The seller can sell them at once to lock the profit or hold them as a view. Long §3.4.1

Dated: specific formulas and quotes

Long's examples use 1990s quotes (0.2% gasoil, high-sulphur fuel oil, London Oil Reports, Telerate). Product specifications tightened (10 ppm diesel, IMO 2020 bunker rules) and PRA assessment methods have changed many times since. Outside the texts The mechanics in this module (averaging, mean of means, fixed or floating differentials, EFPs) remain standard. Confirm current quote names and methodologies in Platts and Argus specification guides before drafting a formula.

Going deeper: options hidden in pricing clauses

Several standard clauses are options that cost one side something. Synthesis

  • Flexible pricing windows give the buyer a choice of averaging period. In a market with a predictable slope (steep contango or backwardation), choosing the cheaper window is worth roughly the curve slope times the shift in time. In a volatile flat market it is closer to a chooser option. Long §3.2.1
  • Trigger pricing gives the trigger holder timing optionality and an incentive to influence the screen just before fixing. That is why it fell out of favour. Long §3.3.2
  • Seller's choice of grade or loading date in forward contracts is a cheapest-to-deliver option, priced into the forward and partly neutralised by escalators. Downey Appendix One
  • Operational tolerance (typically ±1% to ±5% at one party's option) is a small volume option that is worth more when prices move sharply between deal and loading. Downey ch.11

Course 7 values these kinds of embedded options formally. The desk lesson for now: read the formula for who holds which choice.

Key takeaways

  • A formula sets reference, pricing period, differential and adjustments. Draft each one precisely, including what happens when a quote is missing.
  • Back-to-back formula pricing locks a margin; a fixed sale on a formula purchase is an outright price position.
  • Different pricing periods for purchase and sale create exposure even when both legs float. Hedge it with futures across the windows.
  • EFPs, trigger pricing, flexible windows and grade choice are embedded options. Know who holds them.
