Forward, futures and paper markets

Learning objectives

  • Settle a fixed-for-floating swap.

  • Lock a forward Dated price with CFDs.
  • Settle a bookout circle and compute a minimum-variance hedge ratio.

Prerequisites

  • Modules 1–2.

Paper barrels and wet barrels

Forward exposure is traded mostly on paper: exchange futures and OTC swaps whose value derives from physical prices but which rarely end in delivery. Downey puts physical conversion at under 1% of paper volume. The link to physical still matters, because it forces paper and physical prices to converge. Downey ch.18 Trafigura CD ch.10 Downey sorts positions into flat price (outright) and spread positions. Spreads come in four families: cracks (product minus crude), arbitrage spreads (same grade, two locations), relative-value spreads (related grades, one location) and time spreads (same grade, two dates). Downey ch.18

Futures: standard, cleared, margined

  • Futures are standard contracts (for example 1,000 bbl of WTI at Cushing, or Brent) traded on an exchange and cleared by a clearing house. Positions require initial margin and daily variation margin as prices move. Leverage is high: Downey's example is about 14:1. Downey ch.18 Trafigura CD ch.10
  • Exchange clearing removes counterparty risk between traders, but a trader is still exposed to its own broker. Long recommends checking the broker's finances and considering segregated accounts. Long §13.1.2
  • Futures cash flows are not discounted, while swap flows are. Hedging one with the other therefore needs a small volume adjustment. Downey ch.18
Worked example 1 · variation margin on a hedgeChecked by code

A refiner hedges a crude purchase by buying 20 futures lots (1,000 bbl each) at $80.00. Settlements over three days are $78.60, $77.90 and $79.40.

day 1: (78.60 − 80.00) × 20,000 = $−28,000 day 2: (77.90 − 78.60) × 20,000 = $−14,000 day 3: (79.40 − 77.90) × 20,000 = $30,000 cumulative $−12,000

The hedge works economically: the physical purchase is cheaper when futures fall. But the futures losses are paid in cash each day, while the physical benefit arrives only when the cargo prices. Hedging creates liquidity needs, which is one reason trading houses need large credit lines (Course 4). Trafigura CD ch.10–11 Synthesis

OTC swaps

A swap exchanges a fixed price for a floating one, almost always cash-settled against the average of a benchmark over a period (Asian settlement). It is customisable to any published price, volume or period. Downey ch.18 Paying fixed (long the swap) protects a consumer such as an airline; receiving fixed protects a producer. Floating-for-floating swaps hedge spreads, such as jet against crude. Documentation runs from an optional term sheet, through a quick non-binding trade advice, to a binding ISDA-based confirmation. Downey ch.18

Worked example 2 · settling a jet fuel swapChecked by code

An airline pays fixed $735/t on 15,000 t of NWE jet for one month (Downey's structure, own numbers). The month's daily assessments average as four equal weeks of $742, $751, $760 and $755.

floating average = $752.00/t → (752 − 735) = $17.00/t → bank pays the airline $255,000

Only the net amount moves, a few business days after month end. The airline's physical jet cost rose by about the same amount, so its net cost stays near $735. Downey ch.18

Hedging a physical deal: flat price versus basis

Trafigura describes the standard pattern. A trader agrees a sale at benchmark plus a differential, priced at delivery. It sells futures when the deal is struck and buys them back when the sale prices. The futures gain or loss offsets the benchmark's move, leaving the differential margin and basis risk: the physical price and the hedge instrument never move exactly together. Trafigura CD ch.10

Worked example 3 · locking a forward saleChecked by code

A cargo is sold for delivery in two months at benchmark + $1.20. Futures sold today at $82. At pricing, the benchmark is $76.

physical receipt = 76 + 1.20 = $77.20; futures gain = 82 − 76 = $6.00; net = $83.20 = 82 + 1.20 if the grade's differential weakens by $0.40 before pricing: net = $82.80

The hedge locks the benchmark level. It cannot lock the differential, which is the basis risk the desk is paid to manage. Trafigura CD ch.10

The NYMEX WTI Calendar Month Average (CMA) "Merc roll"

US pipeline crude contracts (e.g. Midland WTI, Mars, LLS) trade at a differential to the Calendar Month Average (CMA) of prompt NYMEX Light Sweet crude. However, trading in a NYMEX WTI contract ends three business days before the 25th calendar day of the month preceding delivery (four if the 25th is not a business day), typically around the 19th–22nd. As a result, a 21-trading-day delivery month prices against the prompt futures contract (M1) for only roughly 14 days, and against the second month (M2) for the remaining 7 days (approximately a \(\frac{2}{3} M_1 + \frac{1}{3} M_2\) blend). The difference \(\text{CMA} - M_1 \approx \frac{1}{3}(M_2 - M_1)\) is known on Gulf Coast desks as the Merc roll or calendar roll. Edwards ch.2.3 Outside the texts

Worked example 3b · pricing the NYMEX Merc rollChecked by code

A Houston pipeline trader buys physical WTI for October delivery priced at October NYMEX CMA + $0.85/bbl. In the pricing cycle, 14 trading days trade while November is prompt ($78.00/bbl) and 7 days after expiry trade while December is prompt ($79.50/bbl, a $1.50/bbl contango):

CMA weight M1 = 14 / 21 = 0.667 weight M2 = 7 / 21 = 0.333 CMA benchmark = 0.6667 × 78.00 + 0.3333 × 79.50 = $78.50/bbl Merc roll cost (CMA − M1) = $0.50/bbl invoice price (CMA + $0.85) = $79.35/bbl

In contango, the Merc roll adds positive cost over prompt futures ($0.50/bbl here). A trader hedging CMA crude with M1 futures faces roll basis risk unless they execute a calendar spread trade to roll one-third of the hedge into M2 at expiry. Edwards ch.2.3 Synthesis

Hedge P&L decomposition

Long one cargo priced later, hedged by selling futures

Sign convention: the desk owns the cargo (long physical) and is short futures. Futures P&L is cash settled daily; the physical result arrives when the cargo prices and is paid.

Brent CFDs: hedging a specific pricing week

A cargo priced on Dated in a particular week is exposed to Dated, not to a futures month. Schofield shows the fix. A CFD is a swap on the gap between Dated and the second-month forward for a given week. Buying the CFD and the forward together locks a forward price for Dated in that week: forward Dated = forward price + CFD. Schofield ch.5 Dated-to-frontline swaps (DFLs) do the same against futures over a month. Downey Appendix One

Worked example 4 · locking Dated with a CFDChecked by code

A trader will buy a cargo priced on the average of Dated in a future week (Schofield's method, own numbers). The trader buys that week's CFD at −0.85 and the second-month forward at $81.30.

locked forward Dated = 81.30 − 0.85 = $80.45 at settlement: Dated averages $83.10, CFD settles at −0.60 → CFD gain $0.25 implied forward close = 83.10 + 0.60 = $83.70 → forward gain $2.40 net cost = 83.10 − 0.25 − 2.40 = $80.45

CFD quotes are negative in contango and positive in backwardation. Schofield warns that "widening" and "narrowing" become ambiguous with negative numbers, so always reason in signed differentials. Schofield ch.5

EFPs, EFSs and bookouts

  • EFP swaps a futures position for a physical one, converting flat-price exposure into a spread. EFS does the same between futures and an OTC swap, for example to hedge an illiquid Dubai swap with liquid Brent futures. Downey Appendix One
  • Bookouts. When a chain of forward sales loops back to the same party, the contracts can be cancelled against a base price, with only differences paid. Each party pays its seller volume × (its purchase price − base) and receives the equivalent from its buyer, so each nets its own trading margin. Bankruptcy of one member of the circle complicates this. Long §16.3.9, App. 12.2
Worked example 5 · a four-party bookoutChecked by code

A buys from D at $80.10, B from A at $80.45, C from B at $80.20, and D from C at $80.60, each for 600,000 bbl. The base price is $80.00.

A pays D: 600,000 × (80.10 − 80.00) = $60,000 net results: A $210,000, B $−150,000, C $240,000, D $−300,000 (sum zero)

No oil moves and every party realises exactly its own buy–sell margin. The base price only sets the size of gross payments, and therefore the credit exposure while they are outstanding. Long §16.3.9 Synthesis

ALM analogy

Futures margin is like collateral calls on derivatives that hedge a fixed-rate asset book. The economic hedge is sound, but cash leaves now while the offsetting value sits in an accrual book. Treasury must fund the gap, and accounting must avoid booking the hedge loss without the asset gain. Long notes the tax and accounting push towards matching hedges to physical deals and marking to market. Long §13.1.3 Synthesis

Dated: market structure

Downey and Schofield predate several changes. These include post-2008 OTC reforms (clearing and reporting mandates, margin rules for uncleared swaps under Dodd-Frank and EMIR), the end of open-outcry crude trading, ICE Brent's current cash-settlement design, the move from LIBOR to SOFR for discounting, and position limits on energy futures. Outside the texts Check CME and ICE rulebooks, CFTC and ESMA guidance, and ISDA documentation.

Going deeper: how much to hedge

A one-for-one hedge is natural but not always variance-minimising. If the physical price and the hedge instrument have volatilities σS and σF and correlation ρ, the minimum-variance hedge ratio is

\[ h^{*} = \rho\,\frac{\sigma_S}{\sigma_F}. \]

With ρ = 0.95, σS = $2.20 and σF = $2.00 per bbl over the hedge horizon, h* = 1.045: slightly over-hedge, because the physical grade is a little more volatile than the benchmark. Hull ch.3 In practice desks hedge one-for-one in barrels and manage basis as a separate book. Estimated ratios are noisy, and the basis is where the desk's information advantage lies. Trafigura CD ch.10 Synthesis

Key takeaways

  • Futures are standard, cleared and margined, so hedges need liquidity. Swaps are bespoke, averaged and bilateral.
  • Hedging removes flat-price risk and leaves basis. The differential is what the desk earns and manages.
  • CFDs and DFLs hedge Dated exposure for a specific week or month. EFPs and EFSs convert flat-price exposure into spread exposure.
  • Bookouts settle forward chains in cash. Each party ends with its own margin.
