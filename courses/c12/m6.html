Funding, margin, capital and the xVA desk

Core≈ 60 min8 quiz questions7 flashcards

Learning objectives

  • Compute funding, margin and capital value adjustments from exposure and margin profiles.
  • Compare the xVA stack for an uncollateralised, threshold and fully margined hedge.
  • Quantify the liquidity a hedger needs to meet margin calls.
  • Describe the role of an xVA desk and its charging models.

Prerequisites

  • Modules 4–5; Course 4 Module 5 (credit enhancement).

Beyond counterparty risk

CVA prices default. Since the 2008 crisis, dealers have also priced the cost of funding, margin and capital that a trade consumes over its life, and together these are called xVA. Gregory separates three funding-type charges. FVA is the cost of funding the uncollateralised part of a trade and relates to variation margin. MVA is the cost of funding initial margin. KVA is the cost of the equity capital the trade requires. FVA represents the cost of being undercollateralised and MVA the cost of being overcollateralised. Gregory (xVA) ch.18 (p.530) Which charges apply depends on the collateral terms. An uncollateralised trade carries CVA, FVA and KVA. A collateralised trade carries little CVA and no FVA. A trade with initial margin adds MVA. Gregory (xVA) ch.21 (p.612)

Funding: FVA

The usual explanation runs as follows. A dealer sells an uncollateralised swap to a client and hedges it with a collateralised swap with another bank. When the client trade is in the dealer's favour, the client posts nothing, but the dealer must post margin on the offsetting hedge, and must fund that margin at its own unsecured rate while earning only the overnight rate on it. When the client trade moves against the dealer, the reverse happens and the dealer receives funding. Gregory (xVA) ch.18 (pp.531–532) Gregory's cleaner definition is that funding equals value minus margin. Unsecured derivative assets must be funded; unsecured liabilities provide funding. Gregory (xVA) ch.18 (p.534) The symmetric FVA discounts the expected future value at the funding spread. Split into its parts, the funding cost (FCA) uses EPE and the funding benefit (FBA) uses ENE, which makes it look like CVA with the dealer's funding spread in place of the counterparty's credit spread. Gregory (xVA) ch.18 (pp.535–536)

\[ \text{FCA} = -\sum_i \text{EPE}(t_i)\,\text{FS}(t_{i-1},t_i)\,\Delta t_i, \qquad \text{FBA} = -\sum_i \text{ENE}(t_i)\,\text{FS}(t_{i-1},t_i)\,\Delta t_i \]

J.P. Morgan took a $1.5 billion loss when it adopted FVA in the fourth quarter of 2014, and FVA reporting has since become standard among large banks. Gregory (xVA) ch.18 (pp.530–531)

Capital: KVA

A trade ties up regulatory capital for counterparty credit risk, CVA risk, leverage and any residual market risk. Shareholders expect a return on that capital. KVA is the expected capital profile times the cost of capital, often quoted at 8–10% before tax, summed over the life of the trade. Gregory (xVA) ch.19 (pp.566–568)

\[ \text{KVA} = -\sum_i E[K(t_i)]\;\text{CC}\;\Delta t_i \]

Capital rules differ by bank and method, so KVA is the most subjective of the xVAs. Most banks set it as a return hurdle rather than transfer-pricing it upfront. Gregory (xVA) ch.19 (p.568) Gregory (xVA) ch.21 (p.613) The examples below use a deliberately simple capital model: 8% of an exposure at default equal to 1.4 times EPE. The 1.4 is the regulatory alpha factor of the internal-model method; both numbers are outside the texts. Outside the texts

Initial margin: MVA

The clearing mandate and, from September 2016, the bilateral margin rules require initial margin (IM) to cover a severe move over the margin period of risk. Regulatory IM must be segregated and earns little or nothing, so posting it is a pure cost. The ISDA SIMM model has become the market standard for bilateral IM. Gregory (xVA) ch.20 (pp.591–592) MVA is the expected IM profile times the funding spread. Unlike FVA it is a cost only, because IM received is segregated and cannot be reused. Gregory (xVA) ch.20 (p.594) Rating triggers in some agreements also create contingent margin calls, and liquidity rules make banks pre-fund the outflow from a three-notch downgrade. Gregory (xVA) ch.20 (p.592) The examples approximate IM as a 99% ten-day VaR of the remaining position, the same idea clearing houses use to set futures margin. Jorion ch.5 (p.121) Synthesis

Worked example 1 · the xVA stack on an uncollateralised gas swapChecked by code

The five-year swap from Modules 4 and 5: 100,000 MMBtu a month at $3.00, 35% volatility, a 300 bp counterparty with 60% LGD. The dealer's funding spread is 100 bp and its cost of capital 10%. Discounting is at 4%. The utility posts no collateral.

CVA (Module 5) = $0.186m FCA = Σ EPE × 1% × Δt × DF = $0.069m KVA = Σ (8% × 1.4 × EPE) × 10% × Δt × DF = $0.077m total xVA = $0.332m → 5.53 cents/MMBtu

On an uncollateralised five-year hedge, funding and capital together add more than three-quarters as much again to the credit charge, and the total is about 5.5 cents per MMBtu, close to 2% of the price. That is why dealers quote long-dated swaps to unrated end users noticeably wider than to clearing members. Synthesis

Worked example 2 · the same swap under different CSAsChecked by code

Now compare a $2 million threshold CSA, and a CSA with daily variation margin, no threshold and IM posted by the dealer on its hedge. For the threshold case the collateralised EPE from Module 4 replaces the uncollateralised EPE in CVA, FCA and KVA.

$2m threshold: CVA $0.093m + FCA $0.034m + KVA $0.039m → 2.76 cents/MMBtu VM, no threshold: CVA $0.018m + KVA $0.007m + MVA $0.067m → 1.53 cents/MMBtu initial margin at the start (99%, 10 days) = $2.87m

Collateral moves the cost from credit and capital to funding margin. A threshold halves the stack; full margining cuts it to about 1.5 cents, of which three-quarters is now MVA on nearly $3 million of initial margin. The choice is not free for the client, though: a CSA turns credit risk into liquidity risk on the client's side. Gregory (xVA) ch.20 (pp.593–594) Synthesis

The hedger's side: margin is liquidity risk

For the utility or producer, a CSA swaps the dealer's credit charge for its own liquidity risk. Each move against the hedge brings a margin call that must be met in cash, even though the hedged physical position gains in value and will only turn into cash later. Metallgesellschaft is the case study: a hedge that was sound over ten years produced about a billion dollars of margin calls in a single year. Jorion ch.2 (pp.38–39) Jorion's cash flow at risk is the natural tool: simulate the margin calls along with the business cash flows over the planning horizon. Jorion ch.15 (pp.384–387)

Worked example 3 · margin the utility may have to postChecked by code

If the utility had hedged the five-year swap under daily variation margin, how much might it need to post? Take the 95% change in the swap's value over one and three months.

95% one-month margin call = 1.645 × 100,000 × $3.00 × 35% × 60 × √(1/12) = $2.99m 95% three-month margin call = $5.18m

A $3 million one-month call on a hedge with a notional value of $18 million is why many end users prefer to pay the dealer's credit charge, or to hedge with letters of credit and parent guarantees instead of cash collateral (Course 4). The structurer's job is to find the collateral terms that minimise the client's total cost, including liquidity, not just the dealer's xVA. Synthesis

The xVA desk

Large banks centralise these charges in an xVA desk. It prices the incremental xVA of each new trade, reflects it in valuation, optimises margin and capital usage, and manages the resulting P&L volatility. In effect it runs a cross-asset credit hybrid book with components, such as illiquid credit and cross-gamma, that are hard or impossible to hedge. Gregory (xVA) ch.21 (pp.609–610) Practice has evolved from simply pricing CVA, through reserving and formal accounting, to a hard transfer of P&L from the trading desk to the xVA desk and active hedging. Gregory (xVA) ch.21 (p.611) Under transfer pricing, the originating desk pays the xVA charge upfront and passes it to the client. Under a hurdle regime, the xVA desk only sets a target. Most banks transfer-price CVA, FVA and MVA but treat KVA as a hurdle. Gregory (xVA) ch.21 (pp.612–613) Swindle adds the energy point. Energy CVA desks face higher volatility and thin, wide single-name CDS, so they rebalance more often at higher cost than desks in other asset classes. Swindle ch.16 (pp.429–431)

ALM analogy

The xVA stack is a fund transfer pricing system for derivatives. FVA is the liquidity premium charged by treasury, KVA is the cost-of-capital charge in a risk-adjusted return calculation, and MVA is the cost of encumbered assets. An insurer pricing a guaranteed product does the same thing when it charges for the capital the guarantee consumes and the liquidity buffer its collateral calls require. Synthesis

Dated: rates, margin rules and capital

The examples use a 100 bp funding spread and 4% discounting; actual funding spreads and overnight rates have moved widely since 2020, and the IBOR transition replaced LIBOR with overnight risk-free rates. The bilateral margin rules completed their phase-in in 2022. The Basel III final rules changed CVA and counterparty capital. Check your treasury's funding curve, your IM model (SIMM or the schedule) and current capital rules before quoting xVA. Outside the texts

Going deeper: the xVAs overlap

The xVA terms are not independent, and adding them can double count. If a bank books both a funding benefit (FBA) and DVA on the same uncollateralised liabilities, it counts the benefit of its own credit spread twice, since its funding spread largely reflects its own default risk. CVA, FVA and KVA also interact. A CVA hedge with CDS reduces CVA capital, so it reduces KVA. Initial margin received reduces CVA and KVA but creates MVA for the poster. Gregory (xVA) ch.21 (pp.610–612) Gregory (xVA) ch.20 (pp.593–594) Synthesis This is one reason Gregory places the xVA desk at the centre: only a desk that sees all the components can avoid charging the same risk twice, and can price a change of CSA terms or a restructuring correctly. For an energy origination desk, the practical rule is to price a long-dated hedge as a package: the client's credit, the CSA terms, the margin the client can fund, and the dealer's own costs, then choose the terms that minimise the total. Synthesis

Key takeaways

  • FVA prices funding the uncollateralised value, MVA prices posting initial margin, and KVA prices the capital a trade consumes.
  • For an uncollateralised five-year gas hedge, xVA can approach 2% of the price, and funding and capital are as large as CVA.
  • Collateral shifts cost from credit to funding margin, and from the dealer to the client's liquidity.
  • An xVA desk prices and manages the stack centrally, and the components overlap, so they must be priced together.
