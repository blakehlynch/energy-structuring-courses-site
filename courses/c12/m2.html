Portfolio VaR for energy books

Core≈ 65 min8 quiz questions7 flashcards

Learning objectives

  • Compute delta-normal VaR for a multi-commodity book.
  • Decompose VaR into marginal, incremental and component VaR and find the best hedge.
  • Adjust VaR for option gamma.
  • Explain how mapping illiquid prices to benchmarks hides basis risk, and how to rescale for seasonality.

Prerequisites

  • Module 1; Course 7 Module 6 (hedging hierarchies).

Delta-normal portfolio VaR

For a book of linear positions, VaR follows from the dollar exposures to each risk factor and their covariance matrix. If x is the vector of exposures and Σ the covariance matrix of daily returns, the portfolio's dollar standard deviation is √(x′Σx), and VaR is α times that. Jorion ch.7 (pp.160–162) This is the delta-normal or variance-covariance method. Each position is replaced by its linear exposure to the risk factors, which makes it fast enough to run in real time. Jorion ch.10 (pp.249–250, 260–262)

\[ \text{VaR}_p = \alpha\sqrt{x'\Sigma x}, \qquad \Sigma_{ij} = \rho_{ij}\,\sigma_i\,\sigma_j \]

Jorion distinguishes the individual VaR of each component, their sum (the undiversified VaR) and the diversified portfolio VaR. With long-only positions the undiversified VaR is the worst case, reached when all correlations are one. With longs and shorts the worst case is a correlation of −1, so a spread position that looks almost riskless can be very risky if the correlation breaks. Jorion ch.7 (pp.162–165)

Worked example 1 · VaR of a spark spread bookChecked by code

A desk is long 50,000 MWh of on-peak power at $50 ($2.5 million, 4% daily volatility) and short 375,000 MMBtu of gas at $3.00 ($1.125 million, 3% daily volatility). The correlation is 0.70.

variance = (2.5m × 4%)² + (1.125m × 3%)² − 2 × 0.7 × (2.5m × 4%) × (1.125m × 3%) dollar volatility = $80.09k a day 95% VaR = 1.645 × 80.09k = $131.7k individual VaRs: power $164.5k, gas $55.5k → undiversified $220.0k

The short gas cuts VaR by about a fifth from the power leg's standalone $164.5k, and the diversified figure is 40% below the undiversified sum. The offset depends entirely on the 0.70 correlation, which is far from stable in spikes. Jorion ch.7 (pp.164–165) Synthesis

Marginal, incremental and component VaR

A single VaR number does not say which position to change. Jorion's VaR tools answer that. Jorion ch.7 (pp.166–175)

  • Marginal VaR is the change in portfolio VaR per extra dollar of exposure to a factor: α × cov(Ri, Rp)/σp, which is VaR × βi/W. To cut risk with a fixed amount of trading, cut the position with the largest marginal VaR.
  • Incremental VaR is the change in VaR from a proposed trade. It can be approximated as the marginal VaR vector times the trade, which is fast enough for pre-trade checks.
  • Component VaR is marginal VaR times the current exposure. Component VaRs add up exactly to the portfolio VaR, so they give a decomposition that respects diversification. A negative component is a hedge.
\[ \text{CVaR}_i = x_i \times \frac{\partial\,\text{VaR}_p}{\partial x_i} = \text{VaR}_i\,\rho_{i,p}, \qquad \sum_i \text{CVaR}_i = \text{VaR}_p \]
Worked example 2 · decomposing the spark bookChecked by code
marginal VaR: power 0.0627, gas 0.0223 per $ of exposure component VaR: power 2.5m × 0.0627 = $156.9k (119% of VaR) gas −1.125m × 0.0223 = $−25.1k check: 156.9 − 25.1 = $131.7k = portfolio VaR

Power accounts for more than all of the risk and the short gas is a hedge. The useful question is how much gas to sell. Jorion's best hedge is the extra position in one factor that minimises portfolio variance. Jorion ch.7 (pp.170–171)

best gas position = −ρ × σ_power × x_power / σ_gas = −0.7 × 4% × 2.5m / 3% = $−2.333m = 777.8k MMBtu short (against 375k now) VaR at the best hedge = $117.5k, a 10.8% reduction; gas component VaR = $0

At the variance-minimising hedge the hedge's own component VaR is zero: one more MMBtu either way leaves risk unchanged. A 0.70 correlation can only remove about 29% of the power leg's standalone risk, so most of the VaR is unhedgeable power–gas basis. This is the same minimum-variance ratio as in Course 7 Module 6. Jorion ch.7 (pp.171, 175) Synthesis

Options: delta-gamma and full valuation

Delta-normal VaR treats an option as its delta. For small moves and short horizons that can be adequate, but for large moves or short option positions it can be badly wrong. Adding the second-order term gives the delta-gamma approximation for a monotonic payoff: Jorion ch.10 (pp.252–256)

\[ \text{VaR} \approx |\Delta|\,(\alpha\sigma S) - \tfrac12\,\Gamma\,(\alpha\sigma S)^2 \]

Long gamma lowers VaR and short gamma raises it. For non-monotonic payoffs such as straddles, the worst loss may not come from an extreme move at all, and the portfolio must be fully revalued over many scenarios. Jorion ch.10 (pp.253, 256) Jorion's illustration is Nick Leeson's short Nikkei straddle at Barings. Its delta VaR was close to zero, yet a fall to 17,000 cost about $150 million, and a full Monte Carlo gave a one-month 95% VaR of about $138 million. Jorion ch.10 (pp.257–259)

Worked example 3 · a short-gamma gas options bookChecked by code

A book of sold gas calls has a delta of −400,000 MMBtu and a gamma of −250,000 MMBtu per dollar. Gas is $3.00 with 3% daily volatility.

95% price move = 1.645 × 3% × $3.00 = $0.1480 delta-only VaR = 400,000 × 0.1480 = $59.21k delta-gamma VaR = 59.21k + ½ × 250,000 × 0.1480² = $61.95k

The gamma term adds about 5% here, but it grows with the square of the move, so it matters most in exactly the moves VaR is meant to capture. For books with large short optionality, such as sold swing, tolls with fixed volumes or structured supply deals, use full revaluation. Jorion ch.10 (p.257) Synthesis

What is different about energy books

Swindle identifies three features of energy portfolios that limit standard VaR. Swindle ch.16 (pp.413–414)

  • High dimensionality and illiquidity. A regional power book can have exposure to thousands of prices, only a few percent of which trade regularly. Illiquid marks change in occasional jumps when a trader updates them, and feeding those series into a historical VaR distorts it. Swindle ch.16 (pp.406–407, 412–413)
  • Seasonality. Gas returns are more volatile in winter than in the April and October shoulder months, so a VaR computed on non-seasonal data is wrong at both ends of the year. Swindle ch.16 (p.416)
  • Uncommoditised, "actuarial" residuals such as load and unit-contingent risk, which are better measured by distributions of hold-to-maturity outcomes than by short-horizon VaR. Swindle ch.16 (pp.413–414)

The standard fix for illiquid prices is to map them to a liquid parent in a hedging hierarchy and use the parent's returns. Swindle warns that mapping hides the basis risk without removing it, and that more energy blow-ups have probably come from large basis positions at illiquid points than from outright positions. Swindle ch.16 (pp.405–406, 412–413) His remedy is to project each illiquid exposure onto its liquid parent, by regression or a minimum-variance hedge, compute VaR on that benchmark exposure, and measure the residual specific risk separately as a hold-to-maturity distribution. In his worked example, an Algonquin city-gate winter swap is mapped onto TETM3 for a one-day 95% VaR of about $600,000, with the residual basis risk analysed separately. Swindle ch.16 (pp.414–422)

For seasonality, Swindle rescales each historical return by the ratio of today's seasonal volatility to the seasonal volatility on the day it was observed. Swindle ch.16 (pp.416–417)

\[ \tilde r(t_n) = \frac{s[\theta(t)]}{s[\theta(t_n)]}\,r(t_n) \]
Worked example 4 · rescaling a winter return for a shoulder-month VaRChecked by code

A 6% move was observed in a month whose seasonal volatility factor is 1.06; VaR is being computed today, when the factor is 0.94.

rescaled return = 6% × 0.94 / 1.06 = 5.32%

Without rescaling, a December VaR built on April history understates risk, and an April VaR built on December history overstates it. Swindle ch.16 (pp.416–417) Synthesis

ALM analogy

Component VaR is the same Euler allocation an insurer uses to allocate diversified capital to lines of business: each line's contribution is its standalone risk times its correlation with the total. Mapping illiquid prices to benchmarks is like valuing illiquid private assets with a public-market proxy. It makes the numbers tractable, and it hides the basis risk until the proxy and the asset diverge. Synthesis

Dated: methods in practice

Most energy trading firms now run historical or filtered historical simulation VaR with full revaluation for options, and many report ES alongside it. Bank commodity desks under FRTB must also pass P&L attribution tests, which penalise exactly the proxy mapping Swindle warns about. Check your regulator's FRTB implementation and the risk disclosures in trading-house annual reports. Outside the texts

Going deeper: why the best hedge has zero marginal VaR

Portfolio VaR is a smooth function of each exposure. Adding a little of factor i changes VaR at the rate given by its marginal VaR, which is proportional to its covariance with the portfolio. Jorion's figure shows the VaR curve against the size of one position: the marginal VaR is the slope of the tangent, component VaR is that slope times the position, and the best hedge sits at the bottom, where the slope is zero. Jorion ch.7 (pp.174–175) A hedge at that point is uncorrelated with what is left of the book, so trading it either way can only add risk. On a real desk the hedge also has to be liquid and executable, which is why the hierarchy of Course 7 Module 6 places the benchmark at the top: the liquid hedge is chosen first, and the residual is then managed as basis. Synthesis

Key takeaways

  • Delta-normal VaR is α√(x′Σx); diversification depends on correlations that are least reliable in stress.
  • Component VaRs add up to the portfolio VaR; a negative component is a hedge, and the best hedge has zero marginal VaR.
  • Short gamma raises VaR; non-linear books need delta-gamma or full revaluation.
  • Energy books need seasonal rescaling and separate measurement of illiquid basis risk, because mapping hides it.
