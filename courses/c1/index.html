Course 1 · Foundation

Petroleum market fundamentals and price drivers

Why do oil prices swing between $20 and $140? Why does a 4% supply loss move price far more than 4%? Why can the same barrel be worth more next month than today, or less? This course builds the economics behind those questions, drawing on McNally's history of boom and bust, Swindle's carry framework, Yergin's geopolitics and the practitioner material in Downey, Long, Clews and Trafigura's guide.

By the end you will be able to

  • Map the oil chain, its players and its units, and explain why trading is mostly about differentials.
  • Read reserves reports, PSC terms and decline curves, and find the marginal barrel on a cost stack.
  • Link inventories to the forward curve and value a cash-and-carry trade.
  • Estimate the price impact of supply shocks after buffers.
  • Explain boom-bust cycles, cartel behaviour and the geopolitical drivers of today's market, and know what in the books is dated.

How this course draws on the books

  • Narrative and economics: McNally (cycles, elasticities, cartels), Kaiser ch.4 (price formation). McNally Kaiser
  • Curve and carry: Swindle ch.2–3, Schofield ch.5. Swindle Schofield
  • Industry and supply: Downey, Long, Clews ch.6, Edwards ch.1.3. Downey Long Clews Edwards
  • Geopolitics: Yergin (to 2020). Yergin
  • Trading practice: Trafigura's guide. Trafigura CD

Modules

  1. The oil value chain and who trades it ≈ 55 min ·
    Upstream, midstream, downstream; who the players are; why trading grew; units and orders of magnitude; the pump-price split.
  2. Upstream supply: reserves, fiscal terms, decline and cost ≈ 70 min ·
    Field life cycle, P90/P50/P10 reserves, royalty versus PSC economics, short- versus long-cycle supply, decline curves and the shale treadmill, the cost stack.
  3. Demand, inventories and the forward curve ≈ 70 min ·
    Demand drivers and seasonality, inventories and usable cover, contango and backwardation, the theory of storage, cash-and-carry and floating storage, the Cushing lesson.
  4. Price formation: elasticities, shocks and spare capacity ≈ 60 min ·
    Short- and long-run elasticities, shock arithmetic (linear and non-linear), spare capacity and stocks as buffers, the price corridor, the call on OPEC.
  5. Boom-bust history and market management ≈ 60 min ·
    Standard Oil, the Texas era, OPEC, the post-2008 market and OPEC+; cartel economics; the investment cycle.
  6. Geopolitics and the new map ≈ 55 min ·
    US as exporter, Russia and sanctions, China and chokepoints, the Middle East and spare capacity, 2020, the transition map; heavily flagged as dated.
